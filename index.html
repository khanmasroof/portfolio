    01 Selected case studies

    Five problems, start to finish.

    Each one started as a complaint from sales or marketing. Each one shipped to production, with real users, real money and a CRM that cannot undo a write.

    Case 01 / Telephony

    4,659 calls went missing. The replacement shipped the same day.

    Our phone provider's CRM connector broke silently. Agents kept dialling, outbound logging fell from about 500 calls a day to zero, and nobody's call history reached the CRM for a week.

    Problem
    Without call history, follow-up sequences, lead scoring and manager reviews all stopped working. The vendor confirmed the bug but had no fix date.
    Built
    A webhook service that logs each call about a minute after hang-up, plus a backfill for the outage week. Every call carries its provider id on the lead, so switching back to the vendor later can never log a call twice.
    Rules
    Numbers that match no lead are skipped, never created. Status codes are mapped from the vendor's own historic rows so reports stay comparable.
    90%of the outage week's calls matched to a lead in the dry run, 4,207 of 4,659
    What went wrongThe first live backfill hit the CRM's account-wide daily API cap and paused every other integration for the afternoon. I rebuilt the live path to cost about 1.4 requests per call with a short number cache, and now budget bulk jobs across days.

    Case 02 / Webinars

    The automation was missing 7 of 8 buyers. I replaced it.

    Sales prioritised leads by how much of the webinar they watched and whether they saw the offer. That data came from a no-code automation, and sales had stopped trusting it.

    Found
    The platform only releases attendance when a session ends, so the old automation fired too early. On one class it missed the 85% watcher and 7 of the 8 people who saw the offer. It also posted "watched" for people under the threshold and duplicated activities on repeat visits.
    Built
    The webinar platform has no public API, so the service signs in headlessly, polls every 10 minutes, and re-reads each session for 8 hours to catch late joiners. It writes watch %, a "watched" activity at 50% and above, and Saw Offer or Clicked Offer for the first offer only.
    Handover
    Before posting, it checks what already exists on the lead since the session started, so the switch from the old automation never double-posted.

    Case 03 / Sales tooling

    One queue for everyone who said "yes, call me".

    Prospects could ask for a callback on dozens of forms and on every webinar registration. Each form labelled the question differently, timestamps arrived in two time zones, and two reps would sometimes call the same person.

    Built
    A Callback Requests page that merges every source into one list, newest first, in the team's time zone. Reps press Pick to claim a person, Called when done, or Release to hand them back.
    Design
    I matched on the answer, not the question label, so new forms appear without setup. Staff test entries are filtered out: on launch day they were 29 of the 34 form "yes" answers and would have buried the real ones.
    Access
    Google sign-in, limited to an allowlist the sales manager controls.
    35–40real callback requests a day, in one place, with no double calls

    Case 04 / Web design system

    Forty pages, one system, checked at three widths before every release.

    Marketing launches a new AI program or webinar most weeks. Every page is pasted into a page builder as a single fragment, so I built templates and a release routine that keeps them consistent and fast.

    Pages
    Course landing pages with full-price and discount variants, webinar registration, replay and watch pages, interview-question lead magnets with thank-you pages, and the sitewide header.
    Menu
    The old mega menu scrolled further every time a course was added. I redesigned it as a category rail with a course grid on desktop and a drill-down drawer on mobile, all generated from one course list.

    Case 05 / Personal product

    Inboxly, a shared WhatsApp inbox for small sales teams.

    Everything above, I built for an employer. Inboxly is mine: a team inbox where several agents work one WhatsApp number with assignment, notes and live updates.

    Shape
    A Turborepo monorepo with three apps. The web app is Next.js 15 with TanStack Query. The API is Fastify with JWT auth and Socket.IO for live updates. A worker holds the WhatsApp connection and processes jobs through BullMQ.
    Hard part
    One WhatsApp number must have exactly one live connection. A Redis lock per number makes sure two workers never fight over a session, even during a redeploy.
    Status
    Deployed with web, API, worker, MongoDB and Redis as separate services. The repository is private; I am happy to walk through it.

    03 More work

    Smaller builds, each with one thing I learned the hard way.

    The bottom line of each card is a finding. Vendor documentation is usually where my projects start, and rarely where they finish.

    Lead source dashboard

    Shows marketing which channel each of roughly 100 daily leads came from, in the team's time zone.

    Finding: 38% of leads had no UTM. About a third of those were inbound calls and email replies that can never carry one, so I split them out of "direct".

    Website AI agent

    Answers visitor questions about programs and pricing from a crawled copy of the site, and captures interested visitors as leads.

    Finding: caching the knowledge base cuts cost, but it goes stale silently. A refresh now runs after every pricing change.

    Call owner sync

    When a prospect books a career call, the rep who takes it becomes the lead's owner in the CRM automatically.

    Finding: the scheduling API ignores its own event filter. I filter client-side, and match reps by email because the CRM had duplicate names.

    Dialpad call sync

    Logs completed calls from a second phone system, verifying each signed webhook.

    Finding: every call fires twice, once for the agent and once for the office line. Only the agent leg is logged.

    Claude connectors

    MCP servers for the CRM, GA4, Google Ads, Keap, WhatsApp Business and the webinar platform, so the team can ask questions of live data in plain English.

    Finding: two GA4 properties existed and one recorded nothing. Picking the wrong one returns zeros, not an error.

    Webinar Q&A inbox

    Collects every question asked during webinars and groups them per person, so a rep sees someone's whole thread before calling.

    Finding: zero dependencies. Node's built-in HTTP and SQLite were enough, which leaves nothing to patch.

    Team password vault

    An invite-only password manager with Google sign-in, a light and dark design system, and key rotation.

    Finding: each secret is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a wrapped data key, so rotating the master key never re-encrypts the vault.

    WhatsApp CRM

    Stages, tags, assignment and follow-ups on top of a linked WhatsApp account. It was the prototype that became Inboxly.

    Finding: many chats arrive with hidden ids. I recovered about 1,400 id-to-number mappings from the session store to name them.

    Phone normalizer

    Cleans phone numbers on new leads so every call sync can find the right person.

    Finding: CRM webhooks fire for leads created over the API, but not for leads updated over it. I poll for those instead.

    04 How I work

    Four habits that keep production calm.

    The people who use my tools are a sales team at 9am, not engineers. These habits come from building for them.

    01

    Ship it writing nothing.

    Our CRM keys cannot delete a record, so every write is permanent. Every service starts with DRY_RUN=true and a report of exactly what it would do. The team approves the report, then I flip one switch.

    02

    Measure the API, not the docs.

    Filters that are ignored, events that fire twice, time stamps that are secretly UTC. I test each vendor against the real account before writing the integration, and write down what I find.

    03

    Design for the person who opens it.

    A queue with three buttons beats a dashboard with thirty charts. I sit with whoever uses the tool, ask what they do first in the morning, and put that on top.

    04

    Leave a switch, not a rewrite.

    Delays, directions, thresholds and cut-over dates all live in configuration. When the business changes its mind, and it does, the change is one variable and a redeploy.
